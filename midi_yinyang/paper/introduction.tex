\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Generation across modalities is posed at one of two distances. Vision
and language lie far apart, and the bridge between them is learned as
an alignment~\cite{clip,flamingo}; text and speech lie near, sharing
content in different form, and the bridge is a
translation~\cite{tts,asr}. At either distance generation was posed
as conditioning, one signal given the other in full. It is moving to
joint production, speech with its text~\cite{moshi}, sound with
video~\cite{mmdiffusion}, where the two signals unfold side by side
and neither is finished before the other begins.

Joint production raises a question conditioning never faces. Each
signal follows its own grammar and needs computation of its own, yet
at every instant what one does must fit what the other is doing.
Existing joint models settle the split by design, sharing everything
but a type token or the input and output
layers~\cite{chameleon,moshi,transfusion}, or giving each modality its
own attention and feed-forward weights~\cite{mot,bagel} or its own
experts~\cite{moma}. Neither choice can be tested where it is made:
paired data across modalities is scarce, so every such model starts
from networks pretrained on one modality and trains only the bridge
on pairs, and the natural control, one model trained from scratch on
the pairs with everything shared, does not exist because the data for
it does not.

We ask the question where the control can be built: two \strms{} of
one modality, melody and chords in symbolic music. A \strm{} shares
its partner's representation and clock but not its grammar. Melody
and chords have what makes two modalities interesting, distinct
content under distinct rules, without what gets in the way: no
translation is needed, since both are note events on one grid. Paired
data is therefore abundant, and the fully shared generator, one
sequence model over the merged note
stream~\cite{cp_transformer,compose_embellish,whole_song}, is not a
hypothetical control but the established design and the yardstick
every other design must beat or explain. With one representation there
is nothing to separate on but the music, so any specialisation a model
learns is specialisation by grammar; and cross-modal pairs reach the
same condition once tokenised into one vocabulary on one
clock~\cite{moshi,audiopalm}, so what holds here holds past the point
of alignment.

Within music the existing generators give up one side each. Lead-sheet
models merge the \strms{} into one token sequence with every parameter
shared~\cite{compose_embellish,mmm,whole_song}, so a \strm{}'s grammar
has nowhere to form; harmonisation and chord-conditioned melody models
fix a direction and give one \strm{} in
full~\cite{yeh2021,sun2021,surprisenet}, so it was never written with
its partner in view. We propose a duet transformer that keeps the
\strms{} distinct while letting them shape each other as they unfold:
one autoregressive model over the \strms{} interleaved frame by frame.
Two components are \strm{}-specific, the attention projections and a
router over a shared, unassigned pool of feed-forward experts, so
which experts serve melody, chords or both is learned rather than
imposed~\cite{moma}. Query slots, one per \strm{}, are trained as
masked predictors of the current frame under random commitment of
either partner; at decode one \strm{} is committed and the other
conditioned on it, the leader alternating frame by frame, so each
\strm{} reads its partner's current frame equally often. One
checkpoint generates both \strms{} together or either given the other.
