\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Generation across modalities is posed at one of two distances. Vision
and language lie far apart, and the bridge between them is learned as
an alignment~\cite{clip,flamingo}; text and speech lie near, and the
bridge is a translation~\cite{tts,asr}. Either way generation was
posed as conditioning, one signal given the other in full; it is
moving to joint production, speech with its text~\cite{moshi}, sound
with video~\cite{mmdiffusion}, neither finished before the other
begins.

Joint production raises a question conditioning never faces: each
signal follows its own grammar and needs computation of its own, yet
at every instant what one does must fit what the other is doing.
Unified multimodal models settle the split by design, sharing every
weight but a type token or the input and output
layers~\cite{chameleon,transfusion,ualm}, or giving each modality, or
each token role, its own attention and feed-forward
weights~\cite{mot,bagel} or experts~\cite{moma}; the synchronous
models~\cite{moshi,mmdiffusion} inherit one of these designs. Neither
choice can be tested where it is made: paired data across modalities
is scarce, so such models start from single-modality pretraining and
train only the bridge on pairs, and the control, one model trained
from scratch on the pairs with everything shared, does not exist
because the data does not.

We ask the question where the control can be built: two streams of
one modality, melody and chords in symbolic music. A stream shares its
partner's representation and clock but not its grammar, so the pair
has distinct content under distinct rules without needing translation:
both are note events on one grid. Paired data is
therefore abundant, and the fully shared generator, one sequence model
over the merged note
stream~\cite{cp_transformer,compose_embellish,whole_song}, is the
established design and the yardstick every other design must beat or
explain. Any specialisation a model learns here is specialisation by
grammar, the condition cross-modal pairs reach once tokenised into one
vocabulary on one clock~\cite{moshi,audiopalm}.

Within music, lead-sheet models share every parameter over one merged
sequence~\cite{compose_embellish,mmm,whole_song} and harmonisation
models give one stream in full and fix the
direction~\cite{yeh2021,sun2021,surprisenet}; neither keeps the
streams distinct while letting them shape each other as they unfold.
Our duet transformer does: one autoregressive model over the streams
interleaved frame by frame, stream-specific in its attention
projections and in a router over a shared, unassigned pool of
feed-forward experts, so which experts serve melody, chords or both is
learned rather than imposed~\cite{moma}. Query slots, one per stream,
are trained as masked predictors of the current frame under random
commitment of either partner, and decoded by committing one stream and
conditioning the other, the leader alternating frame by frame so each
stream reads its partner's current frame equally often.
