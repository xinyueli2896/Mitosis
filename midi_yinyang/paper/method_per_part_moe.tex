\label{sec:permod_moe}

Let $\mathbf{h}_p$ be the $d$-dimensional hidden state at position $p$
of the interleaved sequence and $s_p \in \{x, y\}$ its \emph{stream
label} (the streams alternate by frame; a query token inherits the
label of the stream it predicts). Every transformer block ends in a
sparsely gated mixture-of-experts (MoE) feed-forward layer over a pool
of $E$ experts, each an ordinary two-layer network
$\mathrm{FFN}_i(\mathbf{h}) = \mathbf{W}^{(2)}_i
\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{(1)}_i \mathbf{h})$ with GELU $\sigma$ and inner
width $d_f$.

The conventional layer scores every position with a single $E \times d$
router $\mathbf{G}$,
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\pi}_p = \operatorname{softmax}\bigl(\mathbf{G}\,
  \mathbf{h}_p\bigr) .
  \label{eq:shared_router}
\end{equation}
Because the per-stream attention projections imprint the stream label
into the hidden state, $\mathbf{h}_p = \mathbf{c}_p +
\mathbf{s}_{s_p}$ splits into a content component and a
stream-conditional offset (the ``stamp''), and the logits inherit an
identity term $\mathbf{G}\,\mathbf{s}_{s_p}$ that separates the streams
regardless of content. Our routing analysis
(Sec.~\ref{sec:routing}) attributes most of the shared router's expert
separation to this term: capacity spent re-deriving \emph{which stream
a position belongs to}, a bit the input already carries, rather than
\emph{what it contains}.

We therefore give each stream its own router, $\mathbf{G}^{(x)}$ and
$\mathbf{G}^{(y)}$, both $E \times d$, and score every position with
the router of its own stream:
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\pi}_p = \operatorname{softmax}\bigl(
  \mathbf{G}^{(s_p)}\mathbf{c}_p
  + \mathbf{G}^{(s_p)}\mathbf{s}_{s_p}\bigr).
  \label{eq:permod_router}
\end{equation}
Within the population a given router ever scores, its stamp term is a
\emph{constant} logit offset: it can bias which experts a stream
prefers overall, but cannot make two positions of the same stream route
differently. All routing \emph{variation} within a stream is therefore
content-driven by construction.

The pool itself stays shared and unassigned: both routers score the
same $E$ experts, so which ones a stream recruits --- and whether any
serves both, acting as an integrator --- is learned rather than
imposed. The designed contrast is with hard-partitioned
designs~\cite{moma}, which we ablate by masking each stream's logits to
a disjoint half of the pool: same parameters and activated compute, but
an integrator is no longer representable.

Each position is dispatched to its top-$k$ experts with renormalized
weights, and the mixture is added residually and layer-normalized. Load
is balanced with the auxiliary term of~\cite{fedus2022switch} at weight
$0.01$, computed \emph{within each pool} in the hard-partition arm,
where the all-expert form would demand a uniform load that the masked
routing cannot produce.

The change is cheap and exactly localised. Replacing
Eq.~\eqref{eq:shared_router} with Eq.~\eqref{eq:permod_router} adds one
bias-free $E \times d$ matrix per layer (${\approx}10^{-3}$ of it) and
leaves the expert pool, the $k$ of $E$ activated per position, and
every other computation unchanged, so the three arms compare at
parameter and compute parity. The two routers are initialized as copies
of the pretrained shared router, making the first optimization step
identical to the shared-router model's; they diverge only as their
streams' gradients differ. We use $E = 4$, $k = 2$, $d_f = 3d$.
