\subsection{Role specialization via per-part expert routing}
\label{sec:permod_moe}

Let $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ denote the hidden state at
position $t$ of the interleaved sequence, and let
$\rho_t \in \{\mathrm{mel}, \mathrm{har}\}$ be the \emph{part label}
of that position (melody and harmony frames alternate; the appended
query slots inherit the label of the frame they predict). Every
transformer block ends in a sparsely gated mixture-of-experts (MoE)
feed-forward layer with a pool of $E$ experts
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{FFN}_i(\mathbf{x})
  \;=\;
  \mathbf{W}^{(2)}_i\,
  \sigma\!\bigl(\mathbf{W}^{(1)}_i \mathbf{x}\bigr),
  \qquad i = 1, \dots, E,
  \label{eq:expert}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}^{(1)}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d_f \times d}$,
$\mathbf{W}^{(2)}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d_f}$, and $\sigma$ is
the GELU nonlinearity.

\paragraph{Shared router.}
The conventional layer scores every token with a single router
$\mathbf{G} \in \mathbb{R}^{E \times d}$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}_t = \operatorname{softmax}\bigl(\mathbf{G}\,
  \mathbf{x}_t\bigr) .
  \label{eq:shared_router}
\end{equation}
Because the per-part attention projections imprint the part label
into the hidden state, $\mathbf{x}_t$ decomposes as
$\mathbf{x}_t = \mathbf{c}_t + \mathbf{s}_{\rho_t}$, a content
component plus a part-conditional offset (the ``stamp''), and the
router's logits inherit an identity term
$\mathbf{G}\,\mathbf{s}_{\rho_t}$ that separates the two parts
regardless of content. In our routing analysis
(Sec.~\ref{sec:routing}) this identity term accounts for the
majority of the shared router's expert separation: the router spends
its capacity re-deriving \emph{which part a token belongs to} --- a
bit the input already carries --- rather than \emph{what the token
contains}.

\paragraph{Per-part routers over a shared pool.}
We therefore give each part its own router,
$\mathbf{G}^{(\mathrm{mel})}, \mathbf{G}^{(\mathrm{har})}
\in \mathbb{R}^{E \times d}$, and score every token with the router
of its own part:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}_t = \operatorname{softmax}\bigl(
  \mathbf{G}^{(\rho_t)}\,\mathbf{x}_t\bigr)
  = \operatorname{softmax}\bigl(
  \mathbf{G}^{(\rho_t)}\mathbf{c}_t
  + \mathbf{G}^{(\rho_t)}\mathbf{s}_{\rho_t}\bigr).
  \label{eq:permod_router}
\end{equation}
Within the population a given router ever scores, the stamp term
$\mathbf{G}^{(\rho)}\mathbf{s}_{\rho}$ is a \emph{constant} logit
offset: it can bias which experts a part prefers overall, but it
cannot make two tokens of the same part route differently. All
routing \emph{variation} within a part is thus content-driven by
construction, and the identity bit stops consuming router capacity.

Crucially, the expert pool itself remains \emph{shared and
unassigned}: both routers score the same $E$ experts, so which
experts a part recruits --- and whether any expert serves both parts,
acting as an integrator --- is learned rather than imposed. This is
the designed contrast with hard-partitioned designs
(part-specific experts in the sense of MoMa or VL-MoE), which we
ablate by masking each part to a disjoint half of the pool,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{g}_{t,i} =
  \begin{cases}
    \bigl[\mathbf{G}\,\mathbf{x}_t\bigr]_i, &
      i \in \mathcal{P}(\rho_t),\\[2pt]
    -\infty, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:hardroute}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{mel}) \cap \mathcal{P}(\mathrm{har}) =
\varnothing$: same parameters and activated compute, but an
integrator is no longer representable.

\paragraph{Dispatch and objective.}
Each token is dispatched to its top-$k$ experts
$\mathcal{S}_t = \operatorname{top}\text{-}k(\mathbf{p}_t)$ with
renormalized weights, and the layer output is the residually
connected mixture
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{y}_t = \mathrm{LN}\Bigl(\mathbf{x}_t +
  \sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}_t}
  \frac{p_{t,i}}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{S}_t} p_{t,j}}\,
  \mathrm{FFN}_i(\mathbf{x}_t)\Bigr).
  \label{eq:dispatch}
\end{equation}
Load is balanced with the standard auxiliary term
\cite{fedus2022switch},
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bal}}
  = E \sum_{i=1}^{E} f_i\, P_i,
  \qquad
  f_i = \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\textstyle\sum_t
        \mathbbm{1}\bigl[i = \arg\max_j p_{t,j}\bigr],
  \quad
  P_i = \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\textstyle\sum_t p_{t,i},
  \label{eq:aux}
\end{equation}
computed over the tokens $\mathcal{B}$ of a batch, averaged across
layers, and added to the training loss with weight
$\lambda_{\mathrm{bal}} = 0.01$. (In the hard-partition ablation,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:aux} is computed \emph{within each pool} and averaged;
the all-expert form would demand a uniform load that the masked
routing cannot produce, penalizing that arm for its own
architecture.)

\paragraph{Parity.}
Replacing Eq.~\eqref{eq:shared_router} with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:permod_router} adds one bias-free $E \times d$ routing
matrix per layer ($Ed$ parameters, ${\approx}10^{-3}$ of the layer)
and changes neither the expert pool, the number of experts activated
per token ($k$ of $E$), nor any other computation, so comparisons
against the shared-router and hard-partition arms are at parameter
and compute parity. At initialization the two routers are copies of
the pretrained shared router, making the first optimization step
identical to the shared-gate model's; they diverge only as their
parts' gradients differ. We use $E = 4$, $k = 2$, $d_f = 3d$
throughout.
