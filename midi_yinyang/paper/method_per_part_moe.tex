\label{sec:permod_moe}

Every transformer block ends in a sparsely gated mixture-of-experts
(MoE) feed-forward layer. It receives the attention sub-layer's output
$\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_p$ at position $p$ together with that position's
\emph{stream label} $s_p \in \{x, y\}$, and holds a pool of $E$
experts, each an ordinary two-layer network
$\mathrm{FFN}_i(\mathbf{h}) = \mathbf{W}^{(2)}_i
\sigma(\mathbf{W}^{(1)}_i \mathbf{h})$ with GELU $\sigma$ and inner
width $d_f$. We route with a \emph{stream-specific} router:
$\mathbf{G}^{(x)}$ and $\mathbf{G}^{(y)}$, both $E \times d$, each
scoring only the positions of its own stream,
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\pi}_p
  = \operatorname{softmax}\bigl(\mathbf{G}^{(s_p)}\,
      \tilde{\mathbf{h}}_p\bigr).
  \label{eq:permod_router}
\end{equation}

Each stream therefore has a router of its own, scoring its own
positions and nothing else, so a router's capacity goes to what a
position contains rather than to its stream identity. A shared router
would first have to separate the two streams, a distinction the
per-stream projections have already marked in the hidden state. We
quantify the difference against one in the routing ablation. Both
routers score the same $E$ experts, so expert assignment is learned
rather than imposed as in hard-partitioned designs~\cite{moma}: a
stream may recruit any expert, and one expert may serve both.

Each position is dispatched to its top-$k$ experts with renormalized
weights, the mixture is added residually and layer-normalized, and load
is balanced with the auxiliary term of~\cite{fedus2022switch} at weight
$0.01$. We use $E = 4$, $k = 2$, $d_f = 4d$.
