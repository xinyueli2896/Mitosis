\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

Let $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_T)$ and $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_T)$ be two co-occurring
streams on a common frame grid, with $z_t=(x_t,y_t)$ denoting the
time-aligned token pair at frame $t$. Aligned generation of $x$ and $y$
seeks to model
\begin{equation}
    p(z_{1:T})
    =
    \prod_{t=1}^{T} p(x_t,y_t \mid z_{<t}),
    \label{eq:joint_ar}
\end{equation}
where both components depend on the shared history $z_{<t}$. A standard
autoregressive model further factorizes each frame as
$p(x_t \mid z_{<t})\,p(y_t \mid z_{<t},x_t)$, or in the reverse order,
making one stream condition on the other's already-generated token.

The problem is therefore how to predict two time-aligned tokens jointly
without fixing either stream as first. \sys interleaves the two
streams: $x_t$ and $y_t$ are adjacent input tokens sharing rotary
position $t$ and predict $x_{t+1}$ and $y_{t+1}$, respectively, so the
next frame is predicted at two positions of one pass, from one history,
rather than one component after the other. The per-frame joint
distribution is realized through a \emph{draft-and-denoise} step,
\begin{equation}
p_\theta(z_t \mid z_{<t})
=
\sum_{d_t}
\underbrace{p_\theta(d_t \mid z_{<t})}_{\text{draft}}\,
\underbrace{p_\theta(x_t \mid z_{<t},d_t)\,p_\theta(y_t \mid z_{<t},d_t)}_{\text{denoise}},
\label{eq:draft_denoise}
\end{equation}
where the draft $d_t$ is the leading stream's own next-frame prediction,
$x^*_t$ or $y^*_t$, the leader alternating across frames, and the two
denoise distributions are read out from one forward pass.

\subsection{Joint Draft-and-Denoise Generation}
\label{sec:generation}

\sys computes both the draft and the denoise step of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:draft_denoise} with the network of Sec.~\ref{sec:Duet}
itself, one set of weights serving both as in
Transfusion~\cite{transfusion}, through two \emph{draft tokens}, one
per stream, appended after the history at the rotary position of frame
$t$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}c). A draft token is a token of its own
stream, embedded by that stream's frame encoder and passed through the
same stack and frame decoder as the history; it holds a tentative
value of its component of $z_t$, or a learned mask embedding when
empty, reads its own history, the other stream's history through the
cross-stream pass and the other draft token through the current-frame
pass, and is read by no history position. In training, one frame per
window is drawn at random and its draft tokens are filled as decoding
will fill them: each either holds a draft sampled from the model's own
autoregressive head or is left empty, covering both-filled, one-filled
and both-empty; each draft token is supervised against the true $x_t$
or $y_t$ alongside the autoregressive loss. Decoding uses the
one-filled configuration. At step $t$, with $x$ leading, a first
forward draws next-frame predictions of both components from the
autoregressive heads; the leader's becomes the draft and is written
into its draft token, the other is discarded and its draft token left
empty,
\begin{equation}
    d_t = x_t^{*} \sim p_\theta(\,\cdot \mid z_{<t}).
    \label{eq:draft}
\end{equation}
One \emph{denoise} forward then reads out both draft tokens, the
follower given $d_t$ through the current-frame pass and the leader
re-predicted from its own position, replacing the draft,
\begin{equation}
    x_t \sim p_\theta(\,\cdot \mid z_{<t},\, d_t),
    \quad
    y_t \sim p_\theta(\,\cdot \mid z_{<t},\, d_t),
    \quad
    z_{<t+1} = [z_{<t}, z_t],
    \label{eq:denoise}
\end{equation}
and the roles swap, so $y$ leads at $t{+}1$:
\begin{equation}
    z_{<t}
    \xrightarrow{\mathrm{draft}} d_t
    \xrightarrow{\mathrm{denoise}} z_t
    \xrightarrow{\mathrm{commit}} z_{<t+1}.
    \label{eq:generation_summary}
\end{equation}
\sys is thus autoregressive across steps, produces both components of
a step in one denoise forward, and alternates the leading stream, so
neither is fixed as first.
