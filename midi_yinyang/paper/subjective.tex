\subsection{Subjective evaluation}
\label{sec:subjective}

\textbf{Material.} Five held-out POP909 songs, cropped as in
Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}. For each song, six clips: the continuation of
each co-generation system of Sec.~\ref{sec:systems} and the
ground-truth continuation. All clips of a song are rendered with the
same piano timbre for melody and the same string timbre for chord [X:
clip length; whether the prompt is included].

\textbf{Procedure.} A rater is assigned a song and rates its six clips
[X: order randomised]. Each clip is rated on five axes on a scale of
$1$ to $5$: \emph{consistency}, coherence of the continuation with the
prompt; \emph{structure}, phrase organisation and development over the
clip; \emph{melody--chord fit}, agreement between the two streams;
\emph{musicality}; \emph{creativity} [X: exact question wording]. A
rater may rate further songs. Ratings from a pilot on an earlier
version of the page are excluded.

\textbf{Participants.} 21 raters produced 438 ratings [X:
recruitment]. Four raters assigned one identical score to every clip
on every axis and are excluded; their exclusion changes no
significance result reported below, because a constant rater
contributes only tied pairs. The remaining 17 raters (10 male, 7
female; age 21--55, median 27) report some musical training (11), none
(4), or amateur musicianship (2). They contribute 372 ratings, 62 per
system, 6--30 per rater (median 30); each song is rated by 11--15
raters.

\textbf{Statistics.} Each rater rates every system on a song, so the
ratings form a complete repeated-measures design: the subject is a
rater--song pair ($n = 62$), the within-subject factor is the system
(six levels), and each cell holds one rating. For each axis, and for
the mean over axes, a one-way repeated-measures analysis of variance
(ANOVA) tests the effect of system, with Greenhouse--Geisser correction
of the degrees of freedom and partial $\eta^2$ as effect size.
Post hoc, \sys is compared with each other system by a paired
$t$-test, Holm-corrected over the five comparisons of an axis. A rater
contributes up to five pairs, which are not independent; the analysis
is therefore repeated with the rater as subject ($n = 17$, a rater's
songs averaged per system).

\textbf{Results.} Fig.~\ref{fig:subjective} reports the means. The
effect of system is significant on every axis: $F(5, 305)$ is $16.5$
for consistency, $15.8$ for structure, $17.2$ for melody--chord fit,
$19.6$ for musicality, $10.3$ for creativity and $23.1$ for the mean
over axes, all $p < 10^{-7}$ after correction ($\varepsilon$ between
$0.78$ and $0.88$), with partial $\eta^2$ from $0.15$ (creativity) to
$0.27$ (overall). With the rater as subject the effect remains
significant on every axis, $F(5, 80)$ from $5.5$ to $13.1$, $p \le
0.004$, partial $\eta^2$ from $0.26$ to $0.45$. \sys obtains the
highest mean among the systems on consistency ($3.92$), structure
($3.85$), melody--chord fit ($3.94$), creativity ($3.71$) and overall
($3.81$); the finetuned single-stream model obtains the highest mean on
musicality ($3.77$ against $3.65$). Holm-corrected post-hoc
differences, \sys minus system, over the 62 pairs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Single-stream, scratch ($2.99$ overall): $+0.83$ overall and
    $+0.71$ to $+0.95$ per axis, all $p \le 0.001$.
  \item Whole-Song-Gen ($3.35$): $+0.46$ overall ($p < 0.001$);
    $+0.66$ on consistency ($p < 0.001$), $+0.47$ on structure
    ($p = 0.007$), $+0.48$ on fit ($p = 0.011$), $+0.39$ on musicality
    ($p = 0.021$); $+0.31$ on creativity is not significant
    ($p = 0.094$).
  \item Anticipatory Music Transformer ($3.65$): $+0.16$ overall
    ($p = 0.35$); no axis is significant, the largest difference being
    $+0.31$ on consistency ($p = 0.088$).
  \item Single-stream, finetuned ($3.71$): $+0.10$ overall
    ($p = 0.35$); no axis is significant; $-0.13$ on musicality
    ($p = 0.70$).
  \item Ground truth ($4.24$): $-0.43$ overall ($p < 0.001$);
    $-0.40$ on structure ($p = 0.007$), $-0.45$ on fit ($p = 0.001$),
    $-0.61$ on musicality ($p < 0.001$), $-0.40$ on creativity
    ($p = 0.031$); $-0.26$ on consistency is not significant
    ($p = 0.088$).
\end{itemize}
With the rater as subject, the differences to the scratch single-stream
model remain significant on every axis ($p \le 0.045$) and to
Whole-Song-Gen overall ($+0.42$, $p = 0.009$) and on consistency
($p = 0.005$); the difference to ground truth does not reach
significance on any axis at $n = 17$ ($-0.29$ overall, $p = 0.20$).
The subjective ordering, ground truth, \sys, finetuned single-stream,
Anticipatory Music Transformer, Whole-Song-Gen, scratch single-stream,
agrees with the objective ordering of Table~\ref{tab:e1}. The
difference between \sys and the two external systems is largest on
consistency and smallest on musicality and creativity. The difference
between \sys and the finetuned single-stream model, which shares its
backbone, training songs and tokenisation, is not significant on any
axis.
