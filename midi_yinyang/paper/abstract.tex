\begin{abstract}
Cross-modal pairs lie far apart, as vision and language, or near, as
text and speech, sharing content, not form. Generation is moving from
conditioning to joint production. This raises a question conditioning
avoids: each signal needs its own computation, yet the two must agree
at every instant. Joint models fix the split in advance, sharing
everything or partitioning by modality. Neither can be tested across
modalities against one model trained from scratch on both: paired
data is too scarce. We study two \strms{} of one modality, melody and
chords: one representation, two grammars, abundant paired data and an
established fully shared generator. Our duet transformer, an
autoregressive model over interleaved \strms{}, is \strm{}-specific in
attention projections and its router over a shared, unassigned expert
pool; query slots let each \strm{} read its partner's current frame.
On held-out songs one checkpoint matches shared and cascaded
baselines, keeping both \strms{} coupled.
\end{abstract}
