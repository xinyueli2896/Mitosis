\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $x = (x_1, \dots, x_T)$ and $y = (y_1, \dots, y_T)$ be two streams
observed on a common clock, whose steps we call \emph{frames}, and let
$\mathrm{LM}_x$ and $\mathrm{LM}_y$ be temporal Transformers pretrained
on each. At frame $t$ stream $x$ carries $x_t$ and stream $y$ carries
$y_t$. A local encoder maps $x_t$ to one vector
$\mathbf{h}^{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ and a local decoder predicts it
back from that vector autoregressively, so the transformer between them
advances one vector per frame per stream. Our goal is a single model of
the joint process $p([x_t, y_t] \mid x_{<t}, y_{<t})$, built from the
two pretrained backbones and fitted on a small paired corpus.

In the instantiation we study, $x$ is the melody stream and $y$ the
chord stream, and a frame is one sixteenth note long. At one frame a
stream carries all of its simultaneous notes, up to a fixed polyphony,
each made of a program-id sub-token and a pitch--duration sub-token.
Which stream is which is arbitrary --- no component below is specific
to either role.

\subsection{Interleaving the two streams}
\label{sec:interleave}

We place the two streams in one sequence, alternating by frame,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{h}
  = \bigl[\,\mathbf{h}^{x}_1,\ \mathbf{h}^{y}_1,\
            \mathbf{h}^{x}_2,\ \mathbf{h}^{y}_2,\ \dots\,\bigr],
  \label{eq:interleave}
\end{equation}
indexed by $p = 1, \dots, 2T$. We call $p$ the \emph{position}: an
absolute index into the $2T$ entries, not a time index. Each frame
contributes two entries, frame $t$ at $2t{-}1$ and $2t$, with odd $p$
carrying stream $x$ and even $p$ stream $y$.

The model reads this sequence shifted one frame --- two positions ---
to the right, in the usual autoregressive way. Position $p$ therefore
\emph{holds} what position $p{-}2$ carries, which is its own stream one
frame earlier, since shifting by two preserves parity; and it
\emph{predicts} what position $p$ itself carries: its own stream at
frame $\lceil p/2 \rceil$, which we call $p$'s \emph{prediction frame}.
The rotary embedding is applied at that prediction frame rather than at
$p$, so the two entries of a frame share one rotary phase: simultaneity
is encoded as distance zero, and within-stream distance is the musical
distance the pretrained backbone already knows. Rotating by $p$ instead
would double every musical interval and place a $T$-frame excerpt at
rotary $2T$, outside the range the backbone was trained on.
Interleaving rather than concatenating is what makes $[x_t, y_t]$ a
temporal unit: the two streams share one position axis and one clock,
and neither is a block appended to the other.

\subsection{Attention in a Two-backbone Model}
\label{sec:attention}

\sys runs two backbones side by side. Every attention block therefore
carries two sets of query, key, value and output projections,
$\{\mathbf{W}^{Q}_{s}, \mathbf{W}^{K}_{s}, \mathbf{W}^{V}_{s},
\mathbf{W}^{O}_{s}\}$ for $s \in \{x, y\}$, each applied to the
positions carrying its stream and both initialized from one pretrained
single-stream Transformer: melody and chord are two domains of one
modality, so a single symbolic-music model is already a competent
generator of either. What the block contains at this point is two
independent copies sharing an index, with nothing deciding which
positions of one stream a position of the other may read. The mask
design below supplies that.

What a position may read is stated in stream and frame rather than in
$p$. Writing $s(p)$ for the stream of position $p$ and
$t(p) = \lceil p/2 \rceil$ for its prediction frame, attention is
factored into three passes over the same queries, keys and values,
differing only in the set of keys $\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}(p)$ a query
may read. Each pass is a full attention with its own softmax over its
own set, and returns a readout $\mathbf{u}_{\bullet,p}$.

The first is \emph{intra-stream},
$\mathcal{K}_{\text{intra}}(p) = \{ q : s(q) = s(p),\, t(q) \le
t(p)\}$: a position sees its own stream, causally. On the interleaved
sequence this is attention with a stride of two, and it is exactly what
the pretrained backbone does --- run alone, the block is the
single-stream model, once per stream, unchanged.

The second is \emph{cross-stream},
$\mathcal{K}_{\text{cross}}(p) = \{ q : s(q) \neq s(p),\, t(q) <
t(p)\}$: a position sees the other stream at strictly earlier frames,
the part of the partner that generation has already committed. This is
the term that makes the model joint rather than two models sharing an
index --- the melody position for frame $t$ is conditioned on the
chords decided through $t{-}1$, and the chord position on the melody
--- and it is causal, so it costs a decode nothing.

The third is \emph{within-frame},
$\mathcal{K}_{\text{frame}}(p) = \{ q : s(q) \neq s(p),\, t(q) =
t(p)\}$: the single position of the other stream that predicts the same
frame. It is the one edge an autoregressive model over
Eq.~\eqref{eq:interleave} cannot have, and the only one that is not
causal: the two positions predicting frame $t$ admit each other, in
both directions, which is what lets $x_t$ and $y_t$ be settled together
instead of one after the other. It is nonetheless sound, because of the
shift --- a position with prediction frame $t$ holds its own stream at
$t{-}1$, so the edge exchanges the two streams' \emph{context} for
frame $t$ and not their values at it. Neither prediction observes the
other's outcome.

The three sets are disjoint, and their union is every position with
prediction frame at most $t(p)$ --- by the shift, all content strictly
before frame $t(p)$ in both streams. They partition the context a
position is entitled to rather than carving overlapping windows out of
it, and none of them is learned: they are fixed functions of a
position's parity and index. What is learned is how much of each to
use. Writing $\mathbf{u}_{\bullet}$ for the output of each pass, the
three are recombined per position as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{o}_p = \mathbf{W}^{O}_{s}\bigl(
    \mathbf{u}_{\text{intra},p}
    + g^{c}_{s}(\mathbf{h}_p)\,\mathbf{u}_{\text{cross},p}
    + g^{f}_{s}(\mathbf{h}_p)\,\mathbf{u}_{\text{frame},p}\bigr),
  \label{eq:gates}
\end{equation}
where $g^{c}_{s}, g^{f}_{s} \in (0,1)$ are per-stream scalar gates,
each a sigmoid of a learned linear map of the position's hidden state.
Both are initialised with a large negative bias, so at step zero
$g^{c} \approx g^{f} \approx 0$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:gates} collapses to
$\mathbf{W}^{O}_{s}\mathbf{u}_{\text{intra},p}$: the pretrained
attention, run in parallel over the two disjoint halves of the
sequence, which \sys thus reproduces exactly rather than approximates
at initialisation. The
paired data is therefore spent learning \emph{when} the streams should
read each other, starting from a model that already generates each
stream well, rather than learning both streams and their coupling at
once.

\subsection{Order-free joint prediction}
\label{sec:slots}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:interleave} leaves one ordering problem. Within frame $t$
the interleaved sequence still places $x_t$ before $y_t$, so an
autoregressive decode would draw $x_t$, commit it, and condition $y_t$
on the committed value --- the fixed direction we set out to remove. We
resolve this by appending to the sequence two \emph{query slots}, one
per stream, both assigned prediction frame $t$. They are appended
after every clean position, but are given the rotary index $t{+}1$ ---
the phase frame $t$'s content occupies in the shifted sequence --- so
each slot sits where the frame it predicts belongs rather than at the
end. Each attends to the committed past of both streams and to
\emph{the other slot}, and to nothing else. The positions of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:interleave}, which we call \emph{clean} to distinguish
them from the slots, never attend to a
slot, so the slots stay outside the autoregressive pathway. The two
slots are symmetric by construction: they see the same past and one
another, and neither is downstream of the other.

Frame $t$ is then produced in two passes. The first is a \emph{draft}:
one forward yields $\tilde{x}_t, \tilde{y}_t$ from the autoregressive
heads of the two clean positions whose prediction frame is $t$, already
coupled through $\mathcal{M}_{\text{frame}}$ but with neither value
observed by the other. The second is a \emph{refinement}: the drafts
are encoded into the slots as
\begin{equation}
  \text{slot}_s \;\leftarrow\; \mathrm{enc}(\tilde{s}_t) + \mathbf{q}_s,
  \qquad s \in \{x, y\},
  \label{eq:slotinput}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{q}_s$ a learned per-stream slot embedding, and a second
forward emits the committed $x_t, y_t$ from the two slots. Both slots
are written and read out together, so neither stream ever observes the
other's final value for frame $t$: the frame is negotiated rather than
ordered. The construction admits further passes by re-encoding the
committed estimates; we use one throughout.

Training must put in the slots the same content decoding will, so each
step runs two forwards over the same batch. The first is a no-grad
probe whose autoregressive heads produce the draft, sampled at
temperature $1$ to match the commitment the decode makes. The second
writes Eq.~\eqref{eq:slotinput} into the slots and is supervised, with
cross-entropy on both slots against the true frame $t$ alongside the
ordinary autoregressive loss on the clean positions. The draft is not a
corrupted ground truth but a sample from the model's own predictive
distribution --- wrong in the way the model is wrong --- which is
exactly the state the slots meet at inference and which no corruption
process can manufacture. Both slots are drafted on every step, so
training never visits a state in which one stream is resolved and the
other is not.
