\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $x = (x_1, \dots, x_T)$ and $y = (y_1, \dots, y_T)$ be two streams
sampled on a common clock, and let $\mathrm{LM}_x$ and $\mathrm{LM}_y$
be temporal Transformers pretrained independently on each. A frame
$x_t$ is itself a short sequence of tokens, encoded to a single vector
by a local encoder and decoded back by a local decoder, so that the
model above them operates on one vector per frame per stream; we write
$\mathbf{h}^{x}_t, \mathbf{h}^{y}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ for these
frame embeddings. Our goal is a single model of the joint process
$p([x_t, y_t] \mid x_{<t}, y_{<t})$ built from the two pretrained
backbones and fitted on a small paired corpus.

\subsection{Interleaving two pretrained backbones}
\label{sec:interleave}

\paragraph{Sequence layout.}
We place the two streams in one sequence, alternating by frame,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{h}
  = \bigl[\,\mathbf{h}^{x}_1,\ \mathbf{h}^{y}_1,\
            \mathbf{h}^{x}_2,\ \mathbf{h}^{y}_2,\ \dots\,\bigr],
  \label{eq:interleave}
\end{equation}
so position $p$ carries frame $\lceil p/2 \rceil$ of stream
$x$ if $p$ is odd and of stream $y$ if $p$ is even. The sequence is
shifted one frame to the right in the usual autoregressive way, so the
row at position $p$ \emph{holds} frame $\lfloor p/2 \rfloor - 1$ and
\emph{predicts} frame $\lfloor p/2 \rfloor$; we call the latter its
\emph{prediction frame}. Interleaving rather than concatenating is what
makes $[x_t, y_t]$ a temporal unit: the two streams share one position
axis and one clock, and neither is a block appended to the other.

\paragraph{Stream-specific projections.}
Each stream keeps its own query, key, value and output projections,
$\{\mathbf{W}^{Q}_{s}, \mathbf{W}^{K}_{s}, \mathbf{W}^{V}_{s},
\mathbf{W}^{O}_{s}\}$ for $s \in \{x, y\}$, initialised from the
corresponding pretrained backbone. A position is projected by the
matrices of its own stream, so before any cross-stream term is active
the model computes exactly what the two pretrained backbones computed
separately.

\paragraph{Three attention passes.}
Attention is factored into three key-masked passes over the same
queries, keys and values, differing only in which positions each query
may attend to. For a query at position $p$ with prediction frame
$t = \lfloor p/2 \rfloor$ and stream $s$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item $\mathcal{M}_{\text{intra}}$: positions of the \emph{same}
        stream at prediction frames $\le t$ --- the causal attention
        the pretrained backbone already performs;
  \item $\mathcal{M}_{\text{cross}}$: positions of the \emph{other}
        stream at prediction frames $< t$ --- the partner's committed
        past;
  \item $\mathcal{M}_{\text{frame}}$: the position of the other stream
        with prediction frame exactly $t$ --- the partner's
        \emph{current} frame.
\end{itemize}
The first two are causal; the third is the within-frame edge that makes
co-generation possible at all, and it is symmetric: each stream's row
for frame $t$ sees the other's row for frame $t$, in both directions.
Writing $\mathbf{u}_{\bullet}$ for the output of the corresponding
pass, the three are recombined per position as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{o}_p = \mathbf{W}^{O}_{s}\bigl(
    \mathbf{u}_{\text{intra},p}
    + g^{c}_{s}(\mathbf{h}_p)\,\mathbf{u}_{\text{cross},p}
    + g^{f}_{s}(\mathbf{h}_p)\,\mathbf{u}_{\text{frame},p}\bigr),
  \label{eq:gates}
\end{equation}
where $g^{c}_{s}, g^{f}_{s} \in (0,1)$ are per-stream scalar gates,
each a sigmoid of a learned linear map of the position's hidden state.
Both gates are initialised with a large negative bias, so at step zero
$g^{c} \approx g^{f} \approx 0$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:gates} reduces to the
pretrained backbones running side by side without interaction. The
paired data is therefore spent learning \emph{when} the streams should
read each other, starting from a model that already generates each
stream well --- rather than learning both streams and their coupling at
once.

\subsection{Order-free joint prediction}
\label{sec:slots}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:interleave} leaves one ordering problem. Within frame
$t$ the interleaved sequence still places $x_t$ before $y_t$, so a
purely autoregressive decode would condition $y_t$ on $x_t$ and
reintroduce exactly the fixed direction we set out to remove. We
resolve this with placeholder slots and iterative refinement.

\paragraph{Placeholder slots.}
We append to the sequence a pair of \emph{query slots}, one per stream,
both assigned prediction frame $t$. Their masks are those of
Sec.~\ref{sec:interleave} with one addition and one restriction: each
slot attends to the committed past of both streams, and to
\emph{the other slot} --- and to nothing else. Clean rows never attend
to slots, so the slots cannot leak into the autoregressive pathway.
The two slots are thus symmetric by construction: each sees the same
past and the same one partner, and neither is downstream of the other.

\paragraph{Slot contents and refinement.}
A slot input is a frame embedding plus a learned level embedding
$\mathbf{e}_s(r)$ marking the refinement round $r$. Decoding frame $t$
runs $K{+}1$ rounds:
\begin{align}
  \text{round } r = K:\quad
    &\text{slot}_s \leftarrow \mathbf{m}_s + \mathbf{e}_s(K), \\
  \text{round } r < K:\quad
    &\text{slot}_s \leftarrow
      \mathrm{enc}\bigl(\hat{z}^{\,(r+1)}_s\bigr) + \mathbf{e}_s(r),
  \label{eq:refine}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{m}_s$ is a learned mask embedding and
$\hat{z}^{\,(r+1)}_s$ is the previous round's estimate of stream $s$'s
frame. Both slots are updated \emph{together} at every round from the
partner's previous-round estimate, and only the round-$0$ outputs are
committed. The frame is therefore negotiated rather than ordered: at no
round does either stream observe the other's final value for frame $t$.

\paragraph{Training.}
Training must place in the slots the same kind of content decoding will
put there. We use a single level, $K = 1$, and one level per slot pair,
so both slots always carry the same $r$ and every training state lies
on the diagonal the symmetric decode actually visits; an independent
draw per slot would spend half its examples on leader/follower states
this decode never reaches. Each step runs two forwards. The first is a
no-grad probe whose autoregressive content head yields a draft
$\tilde{z}_s$ for frame $t$ of each stream, sampled at temperature $1$
to match the decode's own commitment; the second forward places
$\mathrm{enc}(\tilde{z}_s) + \mathbf{e}_s(0)$ in the slots and is
supervised, with cross-entropy on both slots' predictions of frame $t$
together with the ordinary autoregressive loss on the clean rows. The
draft state is precisely the input Eq.~\eqref{eq:refine} constructs at
$r = 0$, which the corruption process cannot produce on its own: it is
not a noisy ground truth but a sample from the model's own
distribution, wrong in the way the model is wrong.
