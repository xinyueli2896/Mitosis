\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $x = (x_1, \dots, x_T)$ and $y = (y_1, \dots, y_T)$ be two streams
observed on a common clock, whose steps we call \emph{frames}, and let
$\mathrm{LM}_x$ and $\mathrm{LM}_y$ be temporal Transformers pretrained
on each. At frame $t$ stream $x$ carries $x_t$ and stream $y$ carries
$y_t$; we call each of these a \emph{token} of its stream. A token is
not a single symbol: it has internal structure and variable length. A
local encoder collapses $x_t$ into one $d$-dimensional vector
$\mathbf{h}^{x}_t$, and a local decoder reconstructs
a token from one such vector, emitting its parts one at a time. Each
$\mathrm{LM}$ therefore runs on a fixed-width sequence: one vector per
token, whatever that token contains. Our goal is a single model of
the joint process $p([x_t, y_t] \mid x_{<t}, y_{<t})$, built from those
pretrained backbones and fitted on a small paired corpus.

In the instantiation we study, $x$ is the melody stream and $y$ the
chord stream, so a token is a \emph{melody token} or a \emph{chord
token}. A frame is one sixteenth note long and holds one of each.
The parts of a token are its stream's simultaneous notes at that frame,
up to a fixed polyphony, each note written as a pair of
\emph{sub-tokens}: its program id, and one symbol for its pitch and
quantised duration. A note is
therefore a musical unit but not a computational one: no component of
the model operates on it. That is why we give the name token to the
frame-level vector and not to the note. Assigning melody to $x$ and
chord to $y$ is arbitrary; no component below is specific to either
role. Melody and
chord are two domains of one modality, so here
$\mathrm{LM}_x = \mathrm{LM}_y$: both backbones are instantiated from a
single pretrained symbolic-music model, already a competent generator
of either stream. Nothing in the construction assumes this,
and where the streams are genuinely distinct modalities each backbone
is taken from its own pretrained model.

\subsection{Interleaved Input Streams}
\label{sec:interleave}

We place the two streams in one sequence, alternating by frame,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{h}
  = \bigl[\,\mathbf{h}^{x}_1,\ \mathbf{h}^{y}_1,\
            \mathbf{h}^{x}_2,\ \mathbf{h}^{y}_2,\ \dots\,\bigr],
  \label{eq:interleave}
\end{equation}
where the position $p = 1, \dots, 2T$ indexes the sequence. This
\emph{position} is an absolute index into the $2T$ tokens, not a time
index: each frame contributes two of them, odd $p$ carrying stream $x$
and even $p$ stream $y$, and frame $t$ occupies positions $2t{-}1$ and
$2t$.

The model reads this sequence shifted one frame (two positions) to
the right, in the usual autoregressive way. Position $p$ therefore
\emph{holds} what position $p{-}2$ carries, its own stream's token one
frame earlier, since shifting by two preserves parity; and it
\emph{predicts} what position $p$ itself carries, stream
$s \in \{x, y\}$ at frame $\lceil p/2 \rceil$, which we call $p$'s
\emph{prediction frame}. The rotary embedding is applied at that
prediction frame rather than at $p$, so the two tokens of a frame share
one rotary position: simultaneity is encoded as distance zero.
Interleaving rather than concatenating is what makes $[x_t, y_t]$ a
temporal unit: the two streams share one position axis and one clock,
and neither is a block appended to the other.

\subsection{Attention in a Two-backbone Model}
\label{sec:attention}

\sys uses two parallel backbones with stream-specific projections
$\{\mathbf{W}^{Q}_{s}, \mathbf{W}^{K}_{s}, \mathbf{W}^{V}_{s},
\mathbf{W}^{O}_{s}\}$, $s \in \{x, y\}$, applied to their respective
positions. Both backbones, including their attention projections and
feed-forward layers, are initialized from the pretrained model of their
stream; here, as Sec.~\ref{sec:setup} notes, that is one model copied
twice.
The feed-forward configuration is detailed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:permod_moe}.

We design the attention masks carefully since they define the
dependencies within and between streams. Let $s(p)$ denote the stream
at position $p$ and $t(p) = \lceil p/2 \rceil$ its prediction frame.
Attention comprises three passes over shared queries, keys and values,
each independently normalized over a distinct key set:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{K}_{\text{intra}}(p)
    &= \{\, q : s(q) = s(p),\ t(q) \le t(p) \,\},
    \label{eq:kintra}\\
  \mathcal{K}_{\text{cross}}(p)
    &= \{\, q : s(q) \neq s(p),\ t(q) < t(p) \,\},
    \label{eq:kcross}\\
  \mathcal{K}_{\text{frame}}(p)
    &= \{\, q : s(q) \neq s(p),\ t(q) = t(p) \,\}.
    \label{eq:kframe}
\end{align}
\emph{Intra-stream attention} preserves the pretrained backbone's
causal attention independently within each stream, corresponding to a
stride of two in the interleaved sequence. \emph{Cross-stream
attention} conditions each stream on the other at earlier prediction
frames, without requiring an additional sequential decoding step.
\emph{Within-frame attention} connects the two positions predicting the
same frame bidirectionally, supporting concurrent prediction of $x_t$
and $y_t$. Although incompatible with index-based causal masking over
Eq.~\eqref{eq:interleave}, this connection preserves autoregressive
causality, because neither of the two positions contains what it
predicts: under the shift, the one predicting $x_t$ holds $x_{t-1}$ and
the one predicting $y_t$ holds $y_{t-1}$. Reading each other exposes
frame $t{-}1$, never frame $t$.

The three sets partition every position whose prediction frame is at
most $t(p)$, and their masks are fixed functions of position parity and
index. What is learned is how much of each readout to admit. With
$\mathbf{h}_p$ the block's input at position $p$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{o}_p = \mathbf{W}^{O}_{s}\bigl(
    \mathbf{u}_{\text{intra},p}
    + g^{c}_{s}(\mathbf{h}_p)\,\mathbf{u}_{\text{cross},p}
    + g^{f}_{s}(\mathbf{h}_p)\,\mathbf{u}_{\text{frame},p}\bigr),
  \label{eq:gates}
\end{equation}
where $g^{c}_{s}, g^{f}_{s} \in (0,1)$ are per-stream \emph{scalars}:
one value per position for each pathway, applied to every dimension of
the readout, so a gate sets how much enters and never what part of it.

Both gate biases are initialised large and negative, so at step zero
the block is \emph{exactly} the pretrained attention, and the paired
corpus is spent on \emph{when} the streams read each other rather than
on how to generate either. The sub-layer closes as
$\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_p = \mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{h}_p + \mathbf{o}_p)$.

\subsection{Order-free joint prediction}
\label{sec:querytok}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:interleave} still places $x_t$ before $y_t$ within frame
$t$, so an autoregressive decode would draw $x_t$, commit it, and
condition $y_t$ on it, the fixed direction we set out to remove. We
append two \emph{query tokens}, one per stream: unlike the
\emph{content tokens} of Eq.~\eqref{eq:interleave} they carry nothing
of their own and exist only to predict frame $t$.

A query token is fixed by two things, the frame it predicts and what is
written into it. Position cannot supply the frame, since both are
appended after the whole sequence, so it is given: drawn at random in
training, the frame in progress when decoding. What is written in is a
draft of that frame,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{query}_s \;\leftarrow\; \mathrm{enc}(\tilde{s}_t)
  + \mathbf{q}_s,
  \qquad s \in \{x, y\},
  \label{eq:queryinput}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{q}_s$ a learned embedding marking the stream. Training
writes there exactly what decoding will: each step runs a no-grad probe
whose autoregressive heads produce the drafts at temperature $1$, then
a supervised pass with cross-entropy on both query tokens against the
true frame $t$, alongside the ordinary autoregressive loss. The draft
is a sample from the model's own distribution rather than corrupted
ground truth, wrong in the way the model is wrong, and no corruption
process manufactures that state.

That frame also fixes what the token may read: the committed past of
both streams, its partner, and nothing else. It sits at rotary phase
$t{+}1$, where frame $t$ falls in the shifted sequence, so it is placed
at the frame it predicts rather than at the end. No content token ever
reads a query token, so the pair stays outside the autoregressive
pathway and neither is downstream of the other.

Decoding frame $t$ therefore takes two passes. A \emph{draft}: one
forward yields $\tilde{x}_t, \tilde{y}_t$ from the autoregressive heads
of the two content tokens predicting frame $t$, coupled through
$\mathcal{K}_{\text{frame}}$ but with neither token observed by the
other. Then a \emph{refinement}: Eq.~\eqref{eq:queryinput} writes those
drafts in and a second forward emits the committed $x_t, y_t$. Both are
written and read out together, so neither stream observes the other's
final token and the frame is negotiated rather than ordered. Further
refinements are available by re-encoding the committed estimates; we
use one.
