\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup_exp}

\subsection{Pretrained backbone}
\label{sec:backbone}

We start from the pretrained foundation model
of~\cite{yinyang}, a RoFormer with the two-level
structure of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}: a three-layer local encoder collapses a frame's
sub-tokens into one vector, a twelve-layer global decoder of width
$d = 768$ runs over those vectors, and a three-layer local decoder
emits the sub-tokens back. It was pretrained as a \emph{single-stream}
model on 357{,}279 filtered files of the Los Angeles MIDI
dataset~\cite{lamd}, where melody and accompaniment arrive as one
merged token stream and only each note's instrument token separates
them. \sys copies this model into both of its streams.

\subsection{Training data}
\label{sec:data}

Training uses POP909~\cite{pop909}, 909 pop songs with a lead melody
and human chord annotations. The melody track gives stream $x$; stream
$y$ is rendered from the chord annotations as a bass note plus up to
four upper tones. Both are quantised to a shared sixteenth-note grid at
120\,BPM, so a frame is one sixteenth and a bar is 16 frames, and both
are tokenised at a polyphony cap of 8, which clips nothing: chord
frames peak at five simultaneous onsets and melody frames at six across
the corpus. Songs whose sorted index is a multiple of ten are held out,
leaving [X] songs to train on. The paired corpus is therefore
smaller than the backbone's pretraining corpus by more than two orders
of magnitude, which motivates adapting the pretrained model rather than
training a joint model from scratch.

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:training}

Each copy keeps the backbone's twelve layers and width, so \sys is a
12-layer stack of the blocks of
Sec.~\ref{sec:attention}--\ref{sec:permod_moe} at $d = 768$, with
$E = 4$ experts, $k = 2$ active per position, and expert width
$d_f = 4d$: the pretrained feed-forward layer is replicated across the
experts, and the cross-stream and within-frame gates start at bias
$-10$, so training begins from the backbone run independently over the
two streams. We train on windows of 384 frames with AdamW and a
one-cycle schedule peaking at [X], the query-token objective
supervising one frame per window, drawn uniformly. [X: steps,
batch size, hardware.]

\subsection{Systems compared}
\label{sec:systems}

Every system is prompted with the same five-bar crops, generates to
the same length, and is sampled three times per song at temperature
$1$, so the comparison is over what the systems do rather than over
what they were given or how hard they were run.

\textbf{Single-stream.} A CP Transformer over one merged token stream,
where stream identity survives only in each note's program token. We
report it finetuned on POP909 from a large-scale pretrained
checkpoint, and trained on POP909 from scratch. This is the closest
matched opponent: the same tokenisation and budget, without the
two-stream construction.

\textbf{Cascade.} A single-stream specialist continues one stream
alone and a conditional model generates the partner from it. The
conditional stage is a function-alignment adapter~\cite{yinyang}: two
copies of the pretrained backbone, one reading the conditioning stream
and one generating the target, linked by gated cross-attention between
their hidden layers. The published models are finetuned on Nottingham;
we refit the adapter on POP909 so that the cascade is not handicapped
by domain, and report both directions. This is how two streams are
obtained from one-stream machinery once the direction is fixed in
advance.

\textbf{External.} Whole-Song-Gen~\cite{whole_song}, a hierarchical
diffusion model that inpaints a whole lead sheet through form,
counterpoint and lead-sheet levels, prompted with the same crops; and
the Anticipatory Music Transformer~\cite{anticipatory}, a
general-purpose model over arrival-time events, which co-generates both
streams but was trained on a different corpus and tokenisation, so it
is reported as an out-of-domain reference rather than a matched
opponent.
