\subsection{Expert-routing ablation}
\label{sec:abl_routing}

We compare four feed-forward designs at equal parameters and activated
compute, all from the same warm start: a \emph{shared} router scoring
every position; \emph{stream-specific} routers over one pool (\sys,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:permod_router}); a \emph{hard-partitioned} pool, each
stream restricted to two experts with the balance term computed within
its pair~\cite{moma}; and a \emph{dense} layer of inner width $8d$,
matching the activated compute of two $4d$ experts. The two
stream-specific routers start as one matrix, so at step~0 the model is
the shared-router model. Routing statistics are read on a fixed
held-out batch (about 770 positions per stream per layer); a second
batch reproduces every pattern. [X: per-arm training budget.]

A shared router separates the streams strongly (mean $\ell_1$
distance $0.59$ of $2$ between the streams' mean routing distributions)
but mostly by stream identity: with the melody token copied into the
chord positions, so that only identity differs, $0.41$ remains, and
swapping the streams' contents moves the expert preferences in none of
the eleven usable layers. Offering the identity as a per-stream bias on
the logits changes nothing (bias deltas at most $0.06$); removing it
from the router's job does, which is what Eq.~\eqref{eq:permod_router}
does. Fig.~\ref{fig:routing}(a) shows the result: expert purity, the
share of melody among the positions an expert wins, rises from nine
specialist cells of 48 to twelve, no expert being assigned to a stream,
while some experts keep serving both. Fig.~\ref{fig:routing}(b) shows
where the freed capacity goes: routing inside a stream follows the
frame's register above a permutation null in nine chord layers of
twelve against five for the shared router, while melody stays near the
ceiling (ten against nine).

Separate routers do not starve experts (Fig.~\ref{fig:load}): none is
dead in either design, teacher-forced or free-running, and deadliness,
one minus the mean smallest expert load relative to $1/E$, is $0.25$
for both under teacher forcing and $0.31$ against the shared router's
$0.36$ on the model's own output, where the stream-specific routers
also keep loads closer to balanced ($\sigma = 0.071$ against $0.085$).

four arms under the protocol of Sec.~\ref{sec:systems} [X: the
hard-partitioned arm is the load-bearing contrast; the dense arm tells
whether routing is load-bearing at all].
