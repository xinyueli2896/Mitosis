Given two autoregressive models $\mathrm{LM}_x$ and $\mathrm{LM}_y$
pretrained independently on streams $x$ and $y$, we construct an aligned
Transformer, \emph{Duet}, that generates the two streams jointly
(Fig.~\ref{fig:model}). Duet reuses the parameters of both models and
learns their interaction from paired data, approximating a joint model
over $z=[x,y]$. Sec.~\ref{sec:formulation} formulates joint temporal
generation, Sec.~\ref{sec:duet} the architecture, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:generation} the draft-and-denoise generation procedure.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

Let $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_T)$ and $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_T)$ be temporally aligned
streams and $z_t=(x_t,y_t)$ the joint variable at step $t$. A joint
model factorizes as
\begin{equation}
    p(z_{1:T})
    =
    \prod_{t=1}^{T} p(x_t,y_t \mid x_{<t},y_{<t}).
    \label{eq:joint_ar}
\end{equation}
A conventional autoregressive model must further serialize each step,
\begin{equation}
    p(x_t,y_t \mid z_{<t})
    =
    p(x_t \mid z_{<t})\,
    p(y_t \mid z_{<t},x_t),
    \label{eq:ordered_factorization}
\end{equation}
or in the reverse order, imposing an asymmetric dependency between two
simultaneous streams. Duet instead treats $z_t$ as one temporal unit:
it keeps the within-stream computation of $\mathrm{LM}_x$ and
$\mathrm{LM}_y$, learns bidirectional interaction from paired data, and
fixes no order within a step. In implementation the streams are
serialized as $[x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2,\ldots]$ with $(x_t,y_t)$ sharing one
rotary position, and prediction is shifted by one joint step, so
$(x_t,y_t)$ predicts $(x_{t+1},y_{t+1})$; the interleaving imposes no
order within $z_t$.

\subsection{Duet: Aligned Transformer}
\label{sec:duet}

Each layer keeps one copy of the pretrained block per stream and aligns
the two in both sub-layers: attention lets the streams read each other
(Fig.~\ref{fig:model}a) and the feed-forward sub-layer routes both over
a shared expert pool (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}b). All pretrained weights are
fine-tuned; the gates and routers are the only new parameters.

\textbf{Attention fusion}\quad Let $\bm{h}^{(l)}_s$, $s\in\{x,y\}$, be
the streams' hidden states at layer $l$, and $(W^Q_s, W^K_s, W^V_s,
W^O_s)$ the projections inherited from $\mathrm{LM}_s$. The information
transferred from source stream $a$ to target stream $b$ is
\begin{equation}
    A_{ab}
    =
    \operatorname{softmax}
    \left(
        \frac{(\bm{h}_b W^Q_b)(\bm{h}_a W^K_a)^\top}{\sqrt{d}}
        + M_{ab}
    \right)
    \bm{h}_a W^V_a,
    \label{eq:duet_attn}
\end{equation}
with queries from the target and keys and values from the source, and
$M_{ab}$ the additive attention mask, $0$ where a query frame may read
a key frame and $-\infty$ elsewhere. $A_{xx}$ and $A_{yy}$ are the
self-attention of $\mathrm{LM}_x$ and $\mathrm{LM}_y$ under their causal
mask. The cross-stream term reads the partner in two masked passes, over
earlier frames and over the current frame, each admitted by a scalar
gate $g\in(0,1)$ computed from $\bm{h}_s$ (drawn as one Cross Attn and
Gate in Fig.~\ref{fig:model}a); the current-frame pass is causal because
under the shift neither position holds what it predicts. Writing
$\bar{s}$ for the other stream,
\begin{equation}
    \bm{o}_s = W^O_s\bigl(A_{ss} + g_{s}\,A_{\bar{s}s}\bigr),
    \qquad
    \tilde{\bm{h}}_s = \mathrm{LN}(\bm{h}_s + \bm{o}_s).
    \label{eq:gates}
\end{equation}
Gate biases start at $-10$, so at initialization each stream computes
exactly its pretrained attention.

\textbf{Mixture of Experts}\quad The feed-forward sub-layer is a pool of
$E$ experts $\mathrm{MLP}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{MLP}_E$, each a copy of a
pretrained feed-forward network, shared by both streams. Each stream
scores the pool with its own router and mixes its top-$k$ experts,
\begin{align}
    \boldsymbol{\pi}_s
    &= \operatorname{softmax}(G_s\,\tilde{\bm{h}}_s),
    \notag \\
    \bm{u}_s
    &= \sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_k(\boldsymbol{\pi}_s)}
       \pi_{s,i}\,\mathrm{MLP}_i(\tilde{\bm{h}}_s),
    \notag \\
    \bm{h}^{(l+1)}_s
    &= \mathrm{LN}(\tilde{\bm{h}}_s + \bm{u}_s),
    \qquad s\in\{x,y\},
    \label{eq:moe}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{T}_k$ selects the $k$ largest routing weights, with the
balancing term of~\cite{fedus2022switch}. No expert is assigned to a
stream, so per-stream specialists and shared experts emerge rather than
being imposed. We use $E=4$, $k=2$. In our setting $\mathrm{LM}_x$ and
$\mathrm{LM}_y$ are the same pretrained symbolic-music Transformer, so
every $\mathrm{MLP}_i$ starts as a copy of one feed-forward network.
When the two streams come from genuinely distinct models, the pool can
instead be seeded with the feed-forward networks of both, each followed
by a lightweight adaptation layer, so that experts of either origin can
be routed to either stream.

\subsection{Joint Draft-and-Denoise Generation}
\label{sec:generation}

Duet generates autoregressively over $z_t$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}c). Two
positions are appended after the history, one per stream, at the rotary
position of frame $t$; each predicts its component of $z_t$ and reads
the history and its partner, and no history position reads them. At
step $t$ one stream leads: its \emph{draft} is sampled from the
autoregressive head and written into its position, the other position
left empty,
\begin{equation}
    x_t^{*} \sim p_\theta(\,\cdot \mid z_{<t}).
    \label{eq:draft}
\end{equation}
One \emph{denoise} forward then updates both positions: the follower
given the draft through the current-frame pass, and the leader
re-sampled from its own position, replacing the draft,
\begin{equation}
    (x_t, y_t) \sim p_\theta(\,\cdot \mid z_{<t},\, x_t^{*}),
    \qquad
    z_{<t+1} = [z_{<t}, z_t],
    \label{eq:denoise}
\end{equation}
and the roles swap, so $y$ leads at $t{+}1$:
\begin{equation}
    z_{<t}
    \xrightarrow{\mathrm{draft}} x_t^{*}
    \xrightarrow{\mathrm{denoise}} z_t
    \xrightarrow{\mathrm{commit}} z_{<t+1}.
    \label{eq:generation_summary}
\end{equation}
Training writes into the appended positions what decoding will, samples
from the autoregressive heads, and supervises both on the true frame
alongside the autoregressive loss. Duet is thus autoregressive across
steps, produces both components of a step in one denoise forward, and
alternates the leading stream, so neither is fixed as first.
